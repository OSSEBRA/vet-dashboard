How to use this dashboard

Quick start

Option 1 — Try the demo

  1. Open the Time series tab.
  2. Click Load demo data.
  3. Review the automatically identified CAP/FLOOR behavior and VET results.
  4. Open Intervention / Three-pipe analysis to see the synthetic before/after example.

The demo dataset is synthetic and does not represent a real district-heating network.

Option 2 — Analyze your own network

  1. Export time-series data from the district-heating monitoring or metering system.
  2. Format the data as CSV.
  3. Upload the CSV in the Time series tab.
  4. Choose the temperature-potential method.
  5. Review VET, η_VET, temperature behavior and circulation.
  6. Use Compare periods or Intervention analysis when appropriate.

Preparing your CSV file

Every row is one measurement/energy interval. Sold energy and pumped volume in a row must cover the same interval. ISO 8601 timestamps are recommended. No fixed timestep is required: hourly, daily and other time-resolved data work when each row is internally consistent.

Basic VET: timestamp, sold_energy_MWh, pumped_volume_m3
Automatic CAP: supply_temperature_C
Automatic historical FLOOR: return_temperature_C, outdoor_temperature_C
Optional / alternative bounds: T_cap_C, T_floor_C, mainly for supplied engineering bounds rather than automatic historical identification.

Units: energy is MWh per interval, pumped volume is m³ per interval, and temperatures are °C. Date-only and date/time values are interpreted consistently in UTC.

Temperature-potential methods

Automatic / Historical

Recommended for operational analysis when sufficient network data are available. T_cap comes from measured supply temperature. T_floor is identified from historical return-temperature behavior relative to outdoor temperature. Historical FLOOR is an empirical operational reference, not a universal physical minimum.

Engineering curve

Use when an operator already has an engineering-defined temperature envelope or target curve.

Manual / Scenario

Use for simple calculations, assumptions and what-if scenarios where T_cap and T_floor are entered manually.

Main outputs

VET [kWh/m³]
Useful sold heat delivered per cubic metre of circulated primary water.
η_VET [%]
Utilization relative to the selected temperature-potential reference.
Measured ΔT
Measured supply minus measured return temperature.
Reference temperature span
T_cap minus T_floor in Automatic / Historical mode.
Pumped primary volume
Primary network water circulated during the selected interval or period.

VET for a period is total sold energy divided by total pumped volume, not the average of interval VET values.

Compare periods

Compare periods evaluates two selected operating periods using the same calculation methodology. Different weather, heat demand and period duration can affect direct comparability.

Intervention / Three-pipe analysis

Use Intervention analysis to compare operation before and after a network change, such as return-side heat utilization or decentralized heat integration. Baseline and after periods should preferably represent comparable seasons and conditions. The historical FLOOR reference is frozen for the formal before/after comparison; post-intervention FLOOR development is shown separately. Measured differences do not by themselves prove causation.

Privacy

Imported datasets are processed locally in the browser and are not uploaded by the dashboard.

Data quality considerations
  • Missing or invalid rows are rejected and reported.
  • Very sparse datasets may not support reliable historical FLOOR identification; Automatic FLOOR requires sufficient observations in each outdoor-temperature bin.
  • Sensor errors and abnormal operating periods can affect results.
  • Longer datasets covering different outdoor conditions generally provide a stronger historical FLOOR reference.

These are practical considerations, not rigid universal requirements.

      Automatic FLOOR methodology

      Historical return temperatures are grouped into 2 °C outdoor-temperature bins. Bins with at least 20 observations use the linearly interpolated 10th percentile as FLOOR; bins below 20 are low-confidence and unsupported. Supported bins are linearly interpolated. Outside the supported range, the nearest FLOOR reference is held and marked as outside calibration. This empirical operational reference is not an absolute thermodynamic minimum or physical maximum.

      Three-pipe interpretation

      Return-side heat utilization can extract useful heat from return-side water without increasing primary circulation. A decentralized heat producer can add useful delivered heat without increasing primary circulation. VET reflects sold energy relative to pumped primary volume: a return-temperature change alone is not rated as deterioration or credited as improved VET. These before/after data do not, by themselves, prove the intervention caused a change.

      About the VET index

      The Volumetric Energy Transfer (VET) index is a network-level indicator of volumetric energy efficiency. It links useful delivered thermal energy to the volume of water circulated in the primary district-heating network and complements existing district-heating KPIs.

      VET = sold heat energy ÷ pumped primary-water volume. For practical inputs, VET [kWh/m³] = sold energy [MWh] × 1,000 ÷ pumped volume [m³]. Energy and volume must refer to the same reporting period. In time-series mode, the same calculation is applied to each interval.

      Temperature-potential reference uses T_cap and T_floor. In Automatic / Historical mode, measured supply temperature at the pumped-volume boundary defines T_cap. Measured return temperature does not directly define T_floor; historical measured return observations grouped by outdoor temperature identify the empirical FLOOR curve. This is not an absolute thermodynamic minimum. The reference span and 1.163 × span [kWh/m³] form a historical reference, not a physical maximum; operation can exceed it and η_VET can be above 100%. Engineering mode uses its imported curve. In Snapshot / Manual mode, entered T_cap and T_floor remain independent of optional diagnostic measured temperatures.

      Utilization η_VET = VET ÷ the volumetric potential associated with the selected bounds. In Automatic / Historical mode, η_VET is utilization relative to the historical temperature-potential reference. It has a thermodynamic connection to temperature utilization, but it is not an exergy-efficiency calculation. No good/bad thresholds are defined here.

      In Snapshot / Manual mode, measured supply and return temperatures are optional diagnostics: they do not set entered T_cap or T_floor, and their difference is reported separately as measured ΔT. Automatic / Historical mode uses measured supply for T_cap and historical return observations to identify the empirical FLOOR curve.
